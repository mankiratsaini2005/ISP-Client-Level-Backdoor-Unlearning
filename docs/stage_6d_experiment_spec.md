# Stage 6D Experiment Specification: Controlled Temporal Influence Study

## 1. Scientific Question
Does the temporal location of malicious client influence within the federated optimization trajectory affect how difficult it is to perform client-level unlearning?

## 2. Hypothesis
Earlier malicious influence may be harder to remove because it has more opportunity to alter subsequent federated optimization (path dependence). *(Note: This is a working hypothesis, not an established fact. The experiment may support, weaken, or falsify it.)*

## 3. Independent Variable
Temporal placement of a fixed number of malicious Client-0 participation events. (Specifically: Early, Middle, Late, with equal malicious participation count and equal poisoned-sample count). 

## 4. Controlled Variables
- Total malicious participations (15).
- Total poisoned samples (3,750).
- Total Client 0 participations overall (50).
- Fixed stochastic client sampling schedule (`client_sampling.json` from Stage 3B).
- CIFAR-10, SmallCNN, 20 clients, 2,500 samples/client, IID partition.
- Optimizer, local epochs, batch size, learning rate, momentum, weight decay.
- Trigger geometry, target class, poison ratio, attack behavior, and random seed.

## 5. Exact Client-0 Event Schedule
Client 0 participates in exactly 50 rounds out of 100 according to the historical schedule:
- Participations 1-15: Rounds 1-30
- Participations 18-32: Rounds 36-56
- Participations 36-50: Rounds 64-100

## 6. Early Condition
Target client acts maliciously *only* during its first 15 sampled participations (Rounds 1-30). In its remaining 35 participations, Client 0 behaves benignly.

## 7. Middle Condition
Target client acts maliciously *only* during 15 middle sampled participations (Rounds 36-56). In its remaining 35 participations, Client 0 behaves benignly.

## 8. Late Condition
Target client acts maliciously *only* during its last 15 sampled participations (Rounds 64-100). In its remaining 35 participations, Client 0 behaves benignly.

## 9. No-Attack Control
Client 0 participates in its original 50 participation events but behaves completely benignly in all 50. No poisoned samples, no attack scaling, no malicious loss, and no attack-specific behavior are applied. This estimates the baseline model behavior when Client 0 is present but non-malicious. **This is NOT the client-excluded counterfactual.**

## 10. Stage-6D matched client-excluded counterfactual ($M_{CF}$)
A single matched client-excluded counterfactual will be trained using:
- same initialization, client partition, sampling schedule, benign clients, optimizer, seed.
- **Client 0 is excluded from every round.**
Because the benign client trajectory is mathematically identical across all timing conditions, this single run serves as the exact matched counterfactual.

## 11. Stage 4 Reference Distinction
The existing Stage 4 model remains a separate global "client-excluded" reference, but it is NOT the exact matched counterfactual for the timing conditions. Stage 4 resampled a 10th client when Client 0 was excluded, thereby fundamentally altering the stochastic sampling trajectory of the 19 benign clients.

## 12. Attack-Impact Analysis
Before analyzing unlearning performance, we compare:
- $M_{Attack}^{Early}$ vs $M_{NoAttack}$
- $M_{Attack}^{Middle}$ vs $M_{NoAttack}$
- $M_{Attack}^{Late}$ vs $M_{NoAttack}$
This isolates the damage caused by malicious behavior itself ($D_{attack}$), separating the magnitude of the initial poisoning effect from the unlearning algorithm's subsequent performance.

## 13. Unlearning Methods
Each unlearning method will strictly start from the compromised model generated by its *own* timing condition.
1. Naive Historical Subtraction
2. Historical Subtraction + KD
3. FedEraser

## 14. Evaluation Protocol
Models will be evaluated on the frozen 7,500 common evaluation set.

## 15. Metrics
- **Security**: ASR (using exact original trigger, plus variants if appropriate).
- **Utility**: Clean accuracy, Clean loss.
- **Counterfactual Fidelity**: Prediction agreement with $M_{CF}$, Output divergence.
- **Parameter Diagnostics**: Final/layer-wise parameter distance.
- **Efficiency**: Unlearning runtime.
- **Cost**: Additional communication, client computation, storage.

## 16. Counterfactual Gap
The primary comparative diagnostic: $E_{cf} = 1 - \text{PredictionAgreement}(M_U, M_{CF})$

## 17. Statistical Limitations
This is a deterministic single-seed exploratory experiment. Therefore, no claims of statistical significance, statistical equivalence, or causal proof will be made at this stage. Findings will be reported as "systematic difference observed", "no clear systematic difference observed", "evidence is suggestive", or "evidence is inconclusive".

## 18. Falsification Criteria
The temporal hypothesis will be weakened or considered unsupported under the tested conditions if no clear systematic timing-dependent difference in counterfactual gap is observed after controlling malicious exposure. This includes if differences are negligible relative to normal reconstruction variability, or if the same method ranking is stable across timing conditions with no meaningful temporal dependence.

## 20. Threats to Validity
- The attack impact might not be sufficient to analyze unlearning if 15 participations yield weak ASR.
- Intermediate checkpoints between $M_{Attack}$ and $M_{CF}$ might not be directly comparable if optimization trajectories drift; trajectory comparisons will only be made if legitimately semantically aligned.

10. Evaluate all outputs.
